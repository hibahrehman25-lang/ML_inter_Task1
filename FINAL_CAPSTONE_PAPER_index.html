CTR & Engagement Opportunity Scoring: Advanced Page Ranking and Decision-Support Framework

Hibah Rehman
FlyRank AI Machine Learning Internship Program (Lane 4 Capstone)
Email: hibahrehman25@gmail.com | Repository: github.com/hibahrehman25-lang/ML_inter_Task1
ABSTRACT Search Engine Optimization (SEO) prioritization at scale requires algorithmic decision support to identify content pages that fail to capture organic traffic despite high visibility. This project asks whether historical search metrics and derived click-through rate (CTR) signals can effectively rank content pages for human review. Using a final temporal modeling population of 136,522 observations and strict client-grouped temporal validation, we construct a Random Forest ranking engine leveraging features such as log-transformed impressions, historical CTR, rank position, and sustained historical presence. The proposed framework achieves an Average Precision (AP) of 0.1376 and a Precision@100 of 0.320 on unseen client test groups. The Random Forest provides a stronger ranking signal than the impression-based baseline, supporting a prioritized human-review queue. The resulting pipeline delivers an evidence-backed action queue that guides manual metadata and content refresh interventions.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

In enterprise web ecosystems, search visibility (impressions) and click engagement do not always align. Web pages frequently rank on search engine results pages (SERPs), collecting significant impressions, yet generate minimal or zero clicks due to unoptimized titles, poor meta descriptions, or search intent misalignment. Manually auditing thousands of URLs to identify these underperforming pages is resource-prohibitive.

Decision Supported by this Work: Which specific content pages should an SEO or editorial team prioritize for manual review and refresh? The system produces a ranked decision-support queue based on historical opportunity patterns. It does not automate content edits, prove causal ranking factors, or guarantee traffic lift.

2. Dataset & Signal Specification

The experiment utilizes the official FlyRank ML Internship warehouse dataset, adhering strictly to public-safe boundaries (hashed identifiers with zero exposure of client domains, credentials, or raw query text).

The initial aggregated page population contains 175,304 observations across 47 client groups. After temporal feature construction and eligibility filtering, the final modeling population contains 136,522 observations across 42 client groups. The opportunity label identifies pages that satisfy the defined visibility condition and record zero clicks in the March outcome window.

In addition to raw search signals, historical Click-Through Rate ($hist\_ctr$) was derived as a key engineering feature to capture click conversion efficiency:

$$\text{hist\_ctr} = \frac{\text{total\_clicks}}{\text{total\_impressions}}$$

3. Methodology & Validation Design

To prevent temporal data leakage, feature matrices are constructed strictly from prior time windows (January–February) to evaluate target outcomes in future windows (March).

Cross-client leakage is controlled using a GroupShuffleSplit evaluation design. The dataset is split by client IDs into a training set (33 clients, 89,530 rows) and an independent held-out test set (9 clients, 46,992 rows). No client appears in both training and test environments.

4. Benchmark Performance Results

Because the positive class is rare (2.51%), classification accuracy is uninformative. Models are evaluated using Precision@K and Average Precision (AP / PR-AUC) to measure ranking quality at the top of the queue.

The final evaluation compares the transparent Impression Baseline with Logistic Regression and Random Forest models on held-out client groups. The Random Forest provides the strongest Average Precision among the evaluated approaches:

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

To adhere to standard academic rigor, the scope and boundaries of these empirical results are explicitly defined:

  • Observational Proxy, Not Ground Truth: Opportunity labels represent an observed historical statistical pattern (high visibility, low clicks), not confirmed business value.
  • No Causal Guarantee: The model ranks pages for review; it does not guarantee that updating meta titles or content will cause search engines to increase rankings or clicks.
  • Expected False Positives: The system is designed as decision support to focus human effort, not automated content deployment.
  • Generalization Scope: Evaluated on 9 held-out clients from a 42-client modeling population; search engine algorithm updates and seasonal variance may require ongoing model retraining.
